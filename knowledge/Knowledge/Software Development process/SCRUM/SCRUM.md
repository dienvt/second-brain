Did you know SCRUM mean a shape in soccer when all team member union into a individual mass and move forward to the goal

**So, why is it called Scrum?** People often ask, “Is Scrum an acronym for something?” and the answer is no. It is actually inspired by a scrum in the sport of rugby. In rugby, the team comes together in what they call a scrum to work together to move the ball forward. In this context, Scrum is where the team comes together to move the product forward.

  

**Scrum** has three pillars: **transparency**, **inspection** and **adaptation**. This supports the concept of working iteratively. Think of Empiricism as working through small experiments, learning from that work and adapting both what you are doing and how you are doing it as needed.

  

# Scrum value

**Commitment** means personally committing to achieving the goals of the Scrum Team.

> As Scrum Master, during Sprint Planning you walk through the work for your next scene and link the Sprint goals to those of the project. The team members agree to do their work within the timeframe, ask for help if it gets challenging, and deliver the scene with the team.

**Courage** means being willing to do the right thing and work through tough problems.

> When necessary, you sometimes need to have direct and difficult conversations so that the team delivers high-quality special effects. Similarly, you need to be confident adjusting your leadership style when you learn that your methods are blocking progress.

**Focus** means concentrating on the necessary work within the Sprint, and the overall goals of the Scrum Team.

> A scene is on a tight deadline. At your Daily Scrum, you encourage team members to emphasize only the most relevant topics to each Sprint, so the team can concentrate on the most applicable information.

**Openness** refers to the team’s willingness to share their challenges that come with performing the work.

> The team ends each Daily Scrum with five minutes of honest discussion. During this discussion, team members voice their challenges and blockers, so that the team can work together to find a solution.

**Respect** is when the team shows consideration for the opinions, skills, and independence of their teammates.

> You encourage teammates to regard each other as equals, even though their levels of experience vary. This helps them listen to each other, especially as they refine the final delivery of the special effects.

  

# Scrum team roles

Product Owner: Build the right thing

Develop teams: Build the thing right

Scrum Master: Build the thing fast
